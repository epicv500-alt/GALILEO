We will not tell you that Galileo is saving the world. We do not think that language is honest, and we do not think it serves you well. What we can say is that every choice we make — from the bark we harvest for The Mat to the paper we use to wrap The Galileo Set — comes from asking whether we are doing this correctly, or whether we are doing it in the way that is merely convenient.

That is not a mission statement. It is just how we work.

## Natural Materials, Chosen Deliberately

Sustainability begins with what a product is made from. Synthetic materials are not always wrong — our soft-touch microfiber lining in The Case is synthetic because nothing natural performs as well in that application — but they are always worth questioning. In almost every other case, we arrived at a natural material because it was genuinely better.

Stainless steel and solid aluminum — the materials of The Vessel and The Case — are among the most recyclable substances used in consumer goods. Brass does not corrode or degrade under normal use. We chose these materials partly because they last, and partly because they do not leave difficult footprints when their useful life eventually ends.

## Handcrafted Processes

Speed and scale are not values we optimize for in production. They produce cheaper objects, but they do not produce better ones.

The Bar is cold-processed — a method that preserves the beneficial oils and proteins in oat that heat-based soap manufacturing would destroy. After processing, each batch is cured for six weeks. This is not standard. Six weeks produces a harder, denser bar that lasts significantly longer in use than soap rushed to shelf in days. The result is a product that is consumed more slowly and generates less waste — not because we told you to use less, but because it is made to go further.

Each bar is then cut by hand. Hand-cutting allows us to maintain the size and weight we intend without the waste of automated trimming, and it means each bar carries the evidence of its making in the way its edges are finished.

<Note>
  The six-week cure is invisible to the person holding The Bar for the first time. But it is the thing that determines how long that bar lasts — and by extension, how often it needs to be replaced.
</Note>

The edge of The Mat is hand-stitched in undyed cotton. Hand-stitching is slower and more expensive than machine finishing, but it allows a level of consistency and tension control that machines cannot reliably provide at the scale we work at. The seam holds because it was put there by someone who could feel whether it was right.

## Packaging Without Performance

The Galileo Set — a pairing of The Bar and The Soak — arrives in undyed packaging sealed with a wax closure.

Undyed means unbleached, uncoated, and unprinted beyond what the packaging requires to identify itself. It does not carry dyes that complicate composting or recycling. The wax seal replaces adhesive tape and ribbon with a single, clean closure that leaves nothing synthetic behind when it is broken. The packaging is not impressive in the way that packaging is often designed to be impressive. It is the right amount of material to do the job it needs to do.

<Tip>
  The undyed outer wrap of The Galileo Set is fully compostable. The wax seal can be removed cleanly before composting — it is not embedded in the paper.
</Tip>

We apply the same thinking across the rest of our packaging. We do not use tissue paper as filler, printed inserts beyond what is necessary, or plastic void fill. If something arrives with less packaging than you expected, that is intentional.

## Designing for Longevity

The most sustainable thing a product can do is not need to be replaced.

This sounds obvious. In practice, it requires resisting a set of incentives that push strongly in the other direction — the incentive to update products seasonally, to introduce marginal changes that make existing versions feel old, to use materials that perform well initially but degrade in ways that drive repurchase.

We do not operate that way. The Mat, The Vessel, The Case, The Bar, The Soak — these are finished products. We are not planning a version two. When we find that a material or process can be improved without compromising what already works, we improve it quietly, without discontinuing what came before.

| Design principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| No seasonal collections | Products are not retired to manufacture urgency |
| Materials that age well | Cork develops patina; steel brushing absorbs marks; brass deepens with handling |
| No planned obsolescence | We do not engineer fragility or design around replacement parts |
| Long-life formulations | Six-week cured soap lasts longer per use; mineral soaks have an indefinite shelf life |

## What We Are Not Claiming

We do not carry certifications we have not earned, and we do not use language that gets ahead of our actual practices. We do not claim carbon neutrality. We do not offset our emissions with purchased credits and present that as equivalence. We have not audited every tier of our supply chain for every material in every product.

What we have done is make honest choices at each point where a choice was available to us — and we continue to ask whether we are doing this correctly.

<Info>
  If you have a specific question about a material's origin, a process, or our supply chain, we would rather answer it directly than point you to a sustainability page that summarizes what we wish were true. Write to us.
</Info>

Sustainability, as we understand it, is not a destination. It is the practice of making things as well as you currently can, and being honest about where you fall short.
